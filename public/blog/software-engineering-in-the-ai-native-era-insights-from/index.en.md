# Software Engineering in the AI-Native Era: Insights from Andrew MacVean and Nicole Forsgren

![Software Engineering in the AI-Native Era: Insights from Andrew MacVean and Nicole Forsgren](/images/blog/software-engineering-in-the-ai-native-era-insights-from.png)

In a recent presentation, Andrew MacVean and Nicole Forsgren of Google's Developer Intelligence Team explored the evolving role of software engineering in the AI-native era. They argue that while AI tools are increasingly writing code, the human role is evolving to focus on higher-level decision-making and system design. 

## The Shift to AI-Native Software Engineering

MacVean and Forsgren start by acknowledging the significant and rapid changes occurring within the software engineering landscape, driven by AI's pervasive adoption. According to the presentation, around 75% of Google's code is now written by AI. Despite AI's significant role in code generation, the authors stress that this isn't about reducing the role of human engineers but rather transforming it. MacVean notes, "This is a big moment for software engineering," emphasizing the rapid pace and magnitude of change.

## Emerging Patterns and Skills for AI-Native Engineers

The presentation outlines several key patterns and skills necessary for engineers to thrive in this new era. MacVean and Forsgren describe an evolution towards a "T-shaped" engineer, where depth in specialized knowledge is complemented by broad skills across the software ecosystem, including AI engagement. "It's non-negotiable that AI-native engineers can really understand the constraints of AI," Forsgren emphasizes.

Key patterns identified include:

1. **Higher Altitudes of Operation**: Engineers must integrate their technical expertise with an understanding of business and user contexts. This requires adjusting to AI's role in execution, focusing on planning and intent.
   
2. **Shifting Left on Intent**: More focus is needed on specifying high-level intent, moving beyond just code to comprehensive planning that includes trade-offs and clear instructions for AI tasks.

3. **System Design**: Engineers are encouraged to design environments where humans and AI agents can collaboratively work towards goals, establishing "guardrails" for AI to ensure quality and reliability.

4. **Team Dynamics and Failure Analysis**: AI is reshaping team structures, giving rise to small, cross-functional teams with less communication overhead. Engineers must remain adaptable, embracing a scientific mindset and being open to experimentation and failure.

5. **Increased Responsibility**: There's a shift from routine code generation to enhancing systems through various AI applications, requiring engineers to manage an ecosystem where agents support the workflow.

## Why This Matters

The insights from MacVean and Forsgren point to a broader conversation on AI's role in industries beyond software engineering. As AI becomes more embedded in various workflows, the emphasis on human creativity, decision-making, and domain knowledge becomes crucial. This shift challenges traditional measures of productivity and prompts a reevaluation of the skill sets required for future readiness.

## Conclusion

MacVean and Forsgren conclude that while AI significantly alters how code is generated and managed, it doesn't diminish the importance of software engineers. Rather, the role is becoming more crucial as engineers navigate and manage complex systems and integrate AI into broader decision-making processes. The presentation reinforces that "the software engineering role is not vanishing; it's simply evolving," with a focus on system-level thinking and strategic decision-making in an AI-driven world.
